# 🌐 Personal Website — Project Plan

> 🎯 **Goal:** publish a simple, credible website quickly, share it in job applications, and improve it based on evidence.  
> **The project is getting a job — not building a perfect website.**

---

## 🚀 1. First version: MVP

> 🟦 **Definition of done:** a live URL, a clear professional story, a downloadable CV, and an easy way to contact me.

- [ ] Choose a simple template / use the HTML-CSS starter.
- [ ] Add my real name and contact details.
- [ ] Add the current CV as a downloadable PDF.
- [ ] Publish the site on a free or low-cost host.
- [ ] Check mobile layout, links, CV download and spelling.
- [ ] Add the URL to selected applications and LinkedIn.

**Timebox:** one focused weekend. Avoid adding features until the first version is live.

## 🧭 2. Positioning

**Working headline:**  
### Software Engineer · Backend, Product & Payments

**Core message:**  
I started in full-stack development and grew into broader technical ownership across payments, integrations and production operations. I want to bring that perspective back into a hands-on engineering role.

**What should stand out**
- Building software across backend, APIs, databases and product features.
- Payments and financial workflows where data integrity and reliability matter.
- Troubleshooting and improving systems in production.
- Understanding both the technical problem and the business context.

**Avoid**
- Presenting myself as only an Operations Manager because of my last title.
- Claiming to be a Node.js specialist or AI engineer without evidence.
- Repeating the CV word for word.
- Exaggerating the scope or impact of work.

## 🧩 3. Three ways into the same profile

The website can have one consistent identity with different emphasis depending on the role. Start with one homepage; add separate pages only when they are useful.

| Track | Initial share of applications* | What to lead with |
|---|---:|---|
| Technical Operations / Integrations / Payment Operations | 50% | Production troubleshooting, APIs, SQL, automation, Stripe and cross-functional problem solving |
| Full-Stack / Backend / Product Engineering | 35% | Hands-on development, Node.js/TypeScript, React, SQL, APIs and product ownership |
| More demanding Payments / Fintech Engineering roles | 15% | Payment flows, reconciliation, data integrity, reliability and technical decisions |

\*These are **starting effort allocations, not measured probabilities of getting hired**. Payments is a domain that can overlap with both Engineering and Operations. Reassess after reviewing real job posts and outcomes.

- [ ] Review recent applications and identify where responses stop.
- [ ] Compare real job descriptions across these three tracks.
- [ ] Adjust the allocation based on response and interview rates.

## ✍️ 4. Storytelling: the useful version of my career

> 💡 **The story is not “I spent five years in my last job.” It is “I started as an engineer, learned to solve business-critical problems, and now want to bring that combination into my next role.”**

Draft direction:

> I started as a full-stack developer, working across backend systems, APIs, databases and product features. Over time, I moved closer to the business problems behind the software: payments, financial workflows, integrations and operational systems.
>
> That broader experience changed how I approach engineering. I care not just about making a feature work, but about understanding the problem, handling edge cases and making sure the solution works reliably in production.
>
> Now I want to bring that combination of hands-on engineering, product thinking and operational experience into my next role — ideally one where I can keep building, deepen my technical skills and take ownership of meaningful problems.

- [ ] Rewrite in my own voice.
- [ ] Prepare a shorter version for recruiters and interviews.
- [ ] Adapt emphasis by track without changing the underlying facts.

## 🏆 5. Evidence to develop

The website should add depth to the CV, not duplicate it. Start with two strong professional examples.

### Candidate case studies
- [ ] **Payment identifier migration** — data integrity, integrations, risk and continuity of payments.
- [ ] **Financial reconciliation / recovery** — investigation, debugging and measurable business impact.
- [ ] **Internal Node.js tools** — automation of operational workflows.
- [ ] **Production incident** — diagnosis, decision-making and verification.

For each case, capture:
- Problem and context
- My contribution versus the team's contribution
- Technical approach and important trade-offs
- Constraints and failure cases
- Verifiable outcome
- What I learned / what I would change today

### Use Cohabs code carefully
- [ ] Review the original code to identify examples I can explain.
- [ ] Confirm what I am allowed to publish or reproduce.
- [ ] Never publish confidential data, credentials, private code or sensitive business logic without permission.
- [ ] Prefer diagrams, sanitized examples or an independent recreation where appropriate.

## 🧪 6. Technical proof: current ability matters

> ⚠️ **A portfolio can get attention, but it cannot compensate for a technical gap in an interview.**

- [ ] Assess current fluency in JavaScript/TypeScript, SQL, APIs, testing and debugging.
- [ ] Practise coding and explain decisions out loud.
- [ ] Build one small **Payments Reliability Lab** with Node.js/TypeScript and PostgreSQL.
- [ ] Include webhooks, idempotency, retries, reconciliation and automated tests.
- [ ] Be able to defend every design decision before featuring it prominently.

This project is for both skill refresh and credible evidence of current hands-on ability.

## 🤖 7. AI: explore honestly, don't overclaim

**Current decision:** don't position myself as an AI specialist yet.

Two separate opportunities:
- **AI for the job search:** experiment with job-post analysis and first drafts of tailored CVs / cover letters. Keep human review and verify every claim.
- **AI as a technical project:** choose a real, bounded problem and demonstrate implementation, testing, failure handling and limitations. Add AI to the public portfolio only if the project strengthens the target profile.

- [ ] Identify one repetitive job-search task worth automating.
- [ ] Measure whether the workflow saves time or improves application quality.
- [ ] Select a small AI project only after defining the problem and success criteria.

## 🎨 8. Design direction

Inspired by the way Mauro's site connects personal positioning, expertise and impact — not by copying his industry or claims.

- Clean, editorial, readable and responsive.
- Strong headline and short personal story.
- Evidence and case studies over decorative animations.
- One clear CV download and contact route.
- First visit should make sense in under two minutes.
- One core identity; tailor emphasis for Engineering, Payments or Technical Operations.

## 📊 9. Measure the job-search impact

Track the funnel, not just website visits.

- [ ] Applications by track and role fit
- [ ] Recruiter responses / screenings
- [ ] Technical interviews
- [ ] Rejections and known reasons
- [ ] CV and website version shared
- [ ] Whether the website was used in the application

Review weekly. If there are no screenings, revisit targeting and positioning. If screens happen but technical interviews fail, prioritize technical readiness and interview practice.
